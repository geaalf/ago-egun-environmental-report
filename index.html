The investigation / 01

Living with a recurring challenge

In Ago-Egun, Ilaje, flooding is not described as a one-off event. It is a recurring part of life for residents.

This report brings together the experiences of community leader Felix Joseph, resident Solakin Babatunde and schoolteacher Mrs Elizabeth. Their accounts point to river overflow and drainage problems as major concerns.

Their stories show how flooding reaches beyond homes, affecting personal belongings, movement and the learning environment.

02

The river and the community

Community leader Felix Joseph said people have lived in the area for more than 75 years. He explained that when the nearby river overflows, the water rises and covers the community.

He said residents and their forefathers have carried out sand-filling activities because the area was once occupied by water. He described river overflow as more severe than flooding associated with rainfall.

“When the river overflows, it rises and covers the entire area.”

Felix Joseph / Community leader

He believes wider and higher drainage channels could help accommodate excess water.

Proposed community intervention
03

The personal cost of flooding

Resident Solakin Babatunde said it is difficult to spend six months in the community without experiencing flooding. He said it commonly occurs around the fifth or sixth month of the year.

Among the personal losses he described was damage to his certificate. He appealed to the government to work on the drainage system.

“You can’t live six months here without flooding.”

Solakin Babatunde / Resident

“The main thing which this flooding has damaged that affected my life is my certificate.”

Solakin Babatunde / Resident
04

When classrooms are affected

Schoolteacher Mrs Elizabeth explained that many pupils in local schools come from the Egun community and are accustomed to the area's water environment.

She said that when it rains, the entire area can become flooded, including classrooms. Teachers wear rain boots to enter classrooms when water makes access difficult. She wants the government to reconstruct and improve the drainage system.

Closing note / 08

The call for action remains.

The accounts gathered for this report show how recurring flooding affects everyday life in Ago-Egun — from damaged personal belongings to difficult access to classrooms. Residents are calling for urgent government intervention, particularly through adequate drainage infrastructure, to help reduce the impact of flooding and improve living conditions.
